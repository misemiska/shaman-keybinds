How macros work

What a macro is

A macro is a short list of commands that the game runs, from top to bottom, when you press its button. Most useful macros are only one or two lines long. You write them in the macro window, which you open with /m, then drag the finished macro onto your action bar like a spell.

#show
/use Healing Wave

#show makes the button show the spell's icon. #showtooltip does the same and also shows the spell's tooltip when you hover over the button, for 7 more characters. /use Healing Wave casts the spell on your current target. On its own that is no better than dragging the spell to your bar, so the interesting part is the conditions.

/use does exactly the same as /cast: /cast can use items and /use can cast spells. /use is one letter shorter, so every macro on this site uses it.

Conditions make it smart

Put conditions in square brackets before a spell name. The spell is only used if everything inside the brackets is true right now.

/use [help]Healing Wave;Lightning Bolt

Read it as: if your target is friendly, cast Healing Wave. Otherwise cast Lightning Bolt. The semicolon separates the choices. The macro goes through them from left to right, uses the first one that fits, and ignores the rest.

  • The last choice is the "otherwise". A spell with no brackets always fits, so put one at the end if you want a fallback. Without one, the button does nothing when no choice fits.
  • One press casts one spell. Only the first choice that fits is used, so the order of your choices matters.

Aim it with @

By default the conditions look at your target. Write @ and a unit to look at, and cast at, someone else. @mouseover is the unit under your mouse, @focus is your focus, @targettarget is whoever your target is targeting, and @player is you.

/use [@mouseover,help,nodead]Healing Wave

The words after the @ are more conditions on that unit, separated by commas, and all of them must be true. help means friendly, harm means an enemy, nodead means alive and dead means dead.

Put two brackets side by side, like [@mouseover,help][help], and either one is enough. That is how a smart healing macro tries one unit after another.

Keys and situations

mod:shift, mod:ctrl and mod:alt check which key you are holding, so one button can do several jobs. combat, swimming and indoors check the situation you are in.

/use [mod:shift]Earth Shock(Rank 1);Earth Shock

Hold Shift for the cheap lower rank, or press normally for your highest rank. Same button, two uses.

To need two keys at once, join their names: [mod:altshift] means Alt and Shift together. mod:shift also fits while you hold Alt and Shift, so put the two-key rule first.

Saving characters

A macro can hold 255 characters, and a button that does four jobs fills that fast. These habits keep a macro short without changing what it does. The builder uses all of them.

  • Leave out the spaces. The game needs no space after ], ; or ,. [help]Healing Wave;Lightning Bolt works exactly like [help] Healing Wave; Lightning Bolt, two characters shorter.
  • Write /use, not /cast, and #show rather than #showtooltip if you can do without the tooltip: 7 characters.
  • Use the short forms. @ instead of target=, mod instead of modifier, btn instead of button. Join two keys into one: mod:altshift is 5 characters shorter than mod:alt,mod:shift.
  • Leave out @target. Conditions look at your target unless you say otherwise, so [help] does what [@target,help] does.
  • Name a spell once. Brackets side by side share the spell after them: [@mouseover,help,nodead][help,nodead]Healing Wave instead of writing Healing Wave twice.
  • Drop conditions that change nothing. The last choice needs no brackets. And if a spell's next choice has no conditions, the brackets before it do nothing: [mod:shift][]Earth Shock is just Earth Shock.
  • Use a spell ID for a long name. 8042 is Earth Shock (Rank 1), 15 characters shorter. Each rank has its own ID: find it in the address of the spell's page on Wowhead, and try it in your game before you rely on it. Small numbers like 16 mean an equipment slot instead, so this only works for spells.

Mistakes that stop a macro working

The game accepts a macro even when part of it can never work, so these mistakes are easy to miss. The builder marks them for you.

  • Check that someone is there. [@mouseover] on its own fits even when nobody is under your mouse, so the macro stops there and never tries the next choice. Add exists, or help or harm, which both mean someone is there. nodead alone doesn't count: nobody there is also "not dead".
  • The macro stops at the first fit, even if the spell then fails. [@mouseover,exists]Lightning Bolt on a friendly player picks Lightning Bolt, the cast fails, and nothing else is tried. Match the spell to who it is for: help for heals, harm for attacks, dead for Ancestral Spirit.
  • A rule below one that always fits is never used. A choice with fewer conditions catches everything a stricter choice below it would. Put the strictest choices first and the "otherwise" last.
  • @cursor is for spells you place on the ground. In WoW: Forever that is Totemic Projection: /use [@cursor]Totemic Projection moves your totems to the spot under your mouse without the targeting circle. TBC Shamans have no such spell.
  • Spells that aren't aimed ignore the @. Shields, imbues and Ghost Wolf always go on you, and totems drop at your feet. The conditions are still checked on that unit, though.

Good to know

  • The limit is 255 characters. Long spell names and many conditions add up. The counter above warns you when you are close, and Saving characters above shows how to win some back.
  • #show or #showtooltip goes first. On its own it shows the spell the macro would cast right now, so the icon changes as your conditions do.
  • If nothing happens, no choice fit. Check the order, and add a spell with no conditions at the end if you want a fallback.
  • Watch out for keys the game already uses. By default, Shift with 1 to 6 switches your action bar page, so a Shift rule on those keys never reaches the macro. Unbind those keys, or use another one.
  • Try a new macro on a friendly player or a training dummy before you rely on it in a fight.
